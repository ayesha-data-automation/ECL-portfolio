IFRS 9 Expected Credit Loss (ECL) Model: Corporate Trade Receivables¶

Project Scope¶

Objective: To calculate the lifetime Expected Credit Loss (ECL) for the corporate trade receivables portfolio using a Roll-Rate (Markov Chain) methodology, in strict compliance with IFRS 9.

Data Scope & Execution Architecture

  • Dataset: five_years_historical_data.xlsx (60 months of historical aging data).
  • Portfolio Segment: Corporate Trade Receivables exclusively.
  • Environment: Complete local execution to guarantee financial data privacy and maintain audit trail integrity.

Core IFRS 9 Policy Decisions

  • Modeling Approach: Simplified Approach (IFRS 9 para 5.5.15). Given the absence of a significant financing component, a Provision Matrix will be utilized to recognize lifetime ECL from day one.
  • Definition of Default: Adhering to the rebuttable presumption established in IFRS 9 (para B5.5.37), default is definitively recognized when balances cross the 90+ days past due threshold.
  • Transition Mechanics: Month-over-month roll rates will be extracted from historical aging buckets to compute the baseline historical cumulative probability of default, which will subsequently be adjusted by Forward-Looking Information (FLI).

Phase 1: ETL Pipeline¶

Objective: To ensure the completeness and structural integrity of the historical aging data before initiating the Expected Credit Loss (ECL) calculations. This phase transforms raw, human-readable financial reports into a machine-readable time-series dataset suitable for Markov Chain modeling.

1.1 Data Extraction

  • Source: Extraction from the 01_Raw_Data folder to maintain audit trail.
  • Target Data: 61 months of historical trade receivable balances, explicitly filtered for the Corporate segment.

1.2 Data Transformation Logic

  • Sanitization: Dynamic column identification and whitespace stripping are applied to prevent missing data errors caused by formatting inconsistencies in the source file.
  • Filtration: The dataset is strictly isolated to valid aging buckets (Current through 120+ Days), excluding irrelevant sub-totals or spacer rows.
  • Transposition: The matrix is inverted to align with standard data science time-series architecture. Time periods (Months) are set as the index (rows), and variables (Aging Buckets) are set as the columns. This structural shift is mandatory for vectorized, month-over-month roll-rate calculations.
  • Type Casting: All balances are explicitly converted to floating-point numbers to guarantee mathematical precision during transition matrix processing.

1.3 Data Load

  • Purpose: To establish a static audit checkpoint. The output applies professional standards (frozen panes, hidden gridlines, firm-standard coloring) so the clean baseline data can be independently verified by reviewers without re-executing the raw extraction pipeline.

Phase 2: Probability of Default Computation¶

Objective: To establish the historical rate at which trade receivables migrate from "Current" status toward default. This phase constructs the core actuarial transition matrix required for the Expected Credit Loss (ECL) model under IFRS 9 (Simplified Approach).

Calculation Logic & Methodology:

  • Step 1: Month-over-Month Transition Rates (Roll Rates) The model calculates the percentage of balances that migrate from one aging bucket to the next consecutive bucket in the following month. It strictly tracks sequential, bucket-to-bucket migrations (rather than aggregate lifecycle leaps) to maintain a mathematically sound Markov Chain.

    Formula: Target_Bucket (Month 2) / Source_Bucket (Month 1)

    Example: To calculate the transition rate for the "Current" bucket in Feb-2020, divide the "0-29 Days" balance in Feb-2020 by the "Current" balance in Jan-2020.

  • Step 2: Terminal State Capping To ensure mathematical integrity, the transition into the terminal default state (90-119 Days $\rightarrow$ 120+ Days) is strictly capped at a maximum of 100%. This prevents outlier recovery anomalies from generating impossible probability metrics ($>1.0$).

  • Step 3: Historical Averaging To eliminate short-term seasonal volatility and establish a reliable baseline, the model computes the arithmetic mean of the month-over-month roll rates across the entire 60-month historical time series.

  • Step 4: Cumulative Probability Chaining (Smoother) The model calculates the lifetime compound probability that an invoice in any given bucket will eventually reach default. In probability math, we calculate a chain of dependent events by multiplying them together.

    Chaining Method (Working Backwards): To build the formulas efficiently in Excel, the model starts at the confirmed point of default and chains the probabilities backward:

    The probability of a "Current" invoice defaulting is the product of successfully tripping every subsequent roll-rate hurdle (Current $\rightarrow$ 0-29 $\rightarrow$ 30-59 $\rightarrow$ 60-89 $\rightarrow$ Default):

    (90-119 Days): An invoice here has already defaulted. Its probability of default (PD) is definitively 100%.

    (60-89 Days): For an invoice here to default, it just needs to roll forward one more time. We calculate this as: Average Roll Rate (60-89) × 100%.

    (30-59 Days): An invoice here must first roll into the 60-day bucket, and then follow the 60-day bucket's path to default. Therefore, we calculate: Average Roll Rate (30-59) × Cumulative PD of the 60-89 bucket.

    This backward multiplication continues all the way down to the "Current" bucket.

    • Result: The final row represents the compound, lifetime probability that a rupee sitting in any given bucket today will eventually migrate all the way to the 90+ day default state.

Phase 3: Loss Given Default (LGD) Computation¶

Objective: To calculate the Loss Given Default (LGD), which represents the historical percentage of a defaulted balance that the firm ultimately fails to recover. Under strict IFRS 9 compliance, this requires isolating true cash recoveries (after default) from administrative, non-cash write-offs (new).

Calculation Logic & Methodology:

  • Step 1: Isolating the Default Pool Adhering to the IFRS 9 rebuttable presumption, the model defines "Default" as any invoice crossing the 90-day threshold. The total default pool is the sum of the 90-119 Days and 120+ Days buckets.

  • Step 2: Write-Off "Add-Back" Logic: (Adjusted Net Movement) When a business writes off a bad debt, the default balance artificially drops without any actual cash being collected. To prevent this from falsely inflating the recovery rate, the model explicitly adds management write-offs back into the closing balance before comparing it to the opening balance.

    Formula: (Closing Default Balance + Write-Offs) - Opening Default Balance

    Audit Rationale: If a Rs. 100,000 bad debt is written off, the system's default balance drops by Rs. 100,000. Without the add-back, a basic formula would assume that Rs. 100,000 was paid in cash (a recovery), which drastically understates the true credit risk.

  • Step 3: Classifying Default Cash Flows The model evaluates the Adjusted Net Movement month-over-month to categorize the change:

    • New Defaults (Positive Movement): Fresh invoice balances entering the 90+ days category.
    • Recoveries (Negative Movement): Actual cash collected against previously defaulted balances.
  • Step 4: Final LGD Computation The model aggregates the historical movements over the 60-month period to establish a smoothed, long-term loss expectation.

    Historical Recovery Rate: Total Cash Recoveries / Total Defaults (including Day 1 Base)

    Loss Given Default (LGD): 1 - Historical Recovery Rate

Phase 4: Historical ECL Provision Matrix¶

Objective: To calculate the final IFRS 9 Expected Credit Loss (ECL) provision by synthesizing the three actuarial components (EAD, PD, and LGD) into a single, audit-ready matrix.

Calculation Logic & Methodology: Under the Simplified Approach for trade receivables, the ECL is recognized as a lifetime expected loss from day one. The model calculates the required provision for each aging bucket using the standard IFRS 9 formula:

ECL = EAD × PD × LGD

  • Exposure at Default (EAD): The outstanding gross carrying amount of the receivables portfolio at the reporting date (the most recent month in the historical dataset).
  • Probability of Default (PD): The historical Cumulative PD (Smoother) derived from the Markov Chain transition matrix in Phase 2.
  • Loss Given Default (LGD): The uniform, write-off LGD calculated in Phase 3, applied across all aging buckets.

Phase 5: Macroeconomic Overlay (Forward-Looking Information)¶

Objective: To align the historical Expected Credit Loss (ECL) with Forward-Looking Information (FLI) as mandated by IFRS 9. This phase introduces a probability-weighted management overlay to adjust historical loss rates based on anticipated future economic conditions.

Calculation Logic & Methodology:

  • Step 1: Scenario Definition & Weighting Management defines three distinct economic scenarios (Base, Optimistic, Pessimistic). Each scenario is assigned a probability weight based on current macroeconomic forecasts (e.g., leveraging State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) economic outlooks or local industry trends). The sum of probabilities must equal 100%.

  • Step 2: Scenario Adjustments A discrete impact multiplier is assigned to each scenario to represent the expected deviation from historical default rates.

    Base Case: 0% deviation (Multiplier = 1.00). Assumes the future mirrors the historical 60-month average.

    Optimistic Case: E.g., -5% deviation (Multiplier = 0.95). Assumes improved liquidity and faster collections.

    Pessimistic Case: E.g., +15% deviation (Multiplier = 1.15). Assumes tighter monetary conditions and higher default risk.

  • Step 3: Weighted Average FLI Scalar The model calculates a single, blended macroeconomic scalar by taking the sum product of the probabilities and their respective multipliers.

    Formula: $\Sigma$ (Scenario Probability $\times$ Scenario Multiplier)

  • Step 4: Final Adjusted ECL The unadjusted historical ECL (calculated in Phase 4) is multiplied by the blended FLI scalar to generate the final, fully compliant IFRS 9 provision.
